04 — FIELD NOTES

Things I've learnt the hard way

01

My first guess at "the problem" was wrong. Twenty stakeholder interviews later, the Aequs project was a completely different — and actually useful — thing. Now I talk to people before I write code.

02

Saying "I don't know that yet" out loud is faster than pretending. Nobody's ever been impressed by a bluff that unravels in the second interview.

03

A prototype that works badly teaches you more in an afternoon than a spec sheet does in a week. Build the ugly version first.

04

Matching students to startups and tuning a control loop are the same problem wearing different clothes. Both collapse the moment you stop listening to the feedback.

05

Ten4teen went from idea to 25+ live partnerships faster than some of my hardware projects went from spec to prototype. Shipping beats polishing.

06

Sleep is not, in fact, an optional feature. I'm told. Working on it.

05 — ALONGSIDE

Ten4teen

I co-founded Ten4teen with Vrushabraj K — a platform connecting startups with engineering students for real project work. It went live publicly on July 1, 2026.

We built it because we kept running into the problem ourselves. Being an engineering student doesn't leave you much room for a full internship, and freelancing sites are so crowded with working professionals that a student barely gets seen. There was a gap between "I can actually do this" and anywhere to prove it — so we made one.

I own product, outreach and partnerships end-to-end, which mostly means learning fast and being the person who fixes it when things break.

We took Ten4teen through YUVA Validate, Deshpande Startups' validation cohort. The real value wasn't the certificate — it was sitting across from that many founders and mentors and coming out with a much clearer idea of what to build next.
